# State of the art in GO prediction around BioReason-Pro / GO-GPT, and its exploitable weaknesses

Conventions. "Paper" = the BioReason-Pro preprint (bioRxiv 2026.03.19.712954, **preprint, not peer reviewed**). "Our numbers" = measurements in `cafa6/REPORT.md` (sections 4.1, 8) and `cafa6/NOTES.md` (GO-GPT benchmark section), produced with `benchmarks/gogpt_eval.py` / `cafa6.bench_gogpt`. ## Q1. What exactly are BioReason-Pro and GO-GPT (architecture, data, vocabulary, decoding, hierarchy use, inputs, compute, reported numbers)?

### Takeaway
GO-GPT is a frozen ESM2 encoder plus a ~200M-parameter prefix-causal GPT decoder that emits a depth-sorted list of GO tokens per aspect, conditioned on one of 200 organism embeddings; it is trained only with teacher-forced cross-entropy on CAFA-5-style experimental labels, ignores the GO graph except through token order, and outputs binary sets. BioReason-Pro is a separate 4B Qwen3 reasoning LLM (ESM3-1B residues + GAT GO-graph memory + text context including GO-GPT's predictions and InterPro) trained by SFT on GPT-5 traces and GSPO/Dr.GRPO RL with an IA-weighted F1 reward; its GO accuracy (0.66 greedy) is only marginally above GO-GPT (0.65) in the paper.

### Cited Findings
**GO-GPT v1 (the checkpoint we benchmark)**
- Encoder: frozen ESM2-3B (`esm2_t36_3B_UR50D`), residue embeddings taken from layer 30 of 36 (intermediate layers at ~80-90% depth worked best); decoder: 12-layer, 12-head GPT adapted from nanoGPT; total ~3.2B parameters (3B ESM2 + ~200M decoder) — [GO-GPT model card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt); [Paper, §4.2.1 and App. B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Prefix-causal attention: residues attend bidirectionally to each other and never to GO tokens; GO tokens attend causally to earlier GO tokens and to all residues; separate QKV/FFN/LayerNorm per stream; sigmoid output gate on attention — [Paper §4.2.1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Vocabulary: flat token per GO term, terms with >=20 training proteins per aspect: 1,470 MF + 7,500 BP + 1,007 CC = 9,977 tokens + 7 delimiters, GO release 2023-01-01. The ~43k-term GO is pruned; the threshold was not ablated "due to lack of sufficient compute" — [Paper §4.2.1, B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Ordering / hierarchy use: within each aspect, terms are sorted by ontology depth (longest root-to-term path) so generation runs general-to-specific; learnable positional embeddings encode "depthwise order". No graph encoder, no hierarchy loss, no consistency constraint in GO-GPT itself; the paper argues the autoregressive factorisation captures hierarchy and cross-aspect dependencies implicitly. Text-initialised GO embeddings gave no gain — [Paper §2.2, §4.2.1-4.2.2, B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Organism: learned embedding for the top-200 training species added to each GO token; other species share an "unknown" embedding. The embedding improved performance and recovers phylogeny — [Paper B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2)
- Inputs: sequence + organism only (no InterPro, structure, PPI or text) — [Paper §2.2, §4.2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Training data: GOA Nov-2022, experimental/curated codes (EXP, IDA, IPI, IMP, IGI, IEP, TAS, IC), propagated over is_a + part_of, CAFA-5 protein/organism composition; proteins annotated before Nov 2022 split **randomly** 90/10 train/val; HF dataset `wanglab/gogpt-training-data` (our count: 119,749 train / 13,305 val) — [Paper §4.1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md)
- Objective/compute: next-token cross-entropy under teacher forcing, AdamW lr 1e-4, cosine, up to 100 epochs, 4x H100, effective batch 160, best checkpoint by validation. Validation loss "plateaus and begins to rise after approximately 20 epochs, indicating overfitting" (Fig. S9) — [Paper §4.2.2, Fig. S9](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Negative results reported by the authors: pretraining on all Swiss-Prot GO did not help; adding electronic (IEA) annotations to training did not help; beam search did not beat greedy/sampling; ESM3 (no structure) slightly worse than ESM2-3B; ESM2-150M clearly worse, ESM2-650M / ESM C 600M / Profluent-E1 600M close to ESM2-3B; decoder 70M-270M gives "small but monotonic" gains — [Paper App. B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Decoding / scores: greedy gives a binary set (prob 1 for generated terms); "probability" mode = term frequency over 10 samples (T=0.7, top-k 20); best-of-10 oracle reported as an upper bound. Released v1 predictor defaults to beam 5, length penalty 0.3, <=300 tokens per aspect — [Paper §4.2.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md); [GO-GPT v2 card ("v1 default decoding: beam search")](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2)
- Test set: temporal holdout, 8,630 proteins gaining experimental annotations Mar 2023-Feb 2024 in an aspect with none before (no-knowledge + limited-knowledge), 26.75 terms/protein; BP 5,819 proteins, MF 2,080, CC 3,440; CAFA-5 IA; official CAFA evaluator — [Paper §4.1.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Reported GO-GPT numbers: wFmax 0.65 greedy vs InterLabelGO+ 0.63 (MF difference n.s., p=0.073); 0.67 with 10-sample frequencies; 0.70 best-of-10 oracle; unweighted Fmax 0.76 vs 0.70. Per-aspect (Table S3, as transcribed in our notes): MF 0.701, BP 0.539, CC 0.713 weighted; 0.766 / 0.587 / 0.801 unweighted — [Paper §2.2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md)
- Our reproduction: released checkpoint scores 0.686 (beam) / 0.687 (greedy) wFmax (MF .714, BP .617, CC .728); the predictions shipped with the HF test set reproduce the paper (0.649), so the public checkpoint is probably not the one behind the paper tables (BP +0.08) — [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md)

**GO-GPT v2 (released ~early Oct 2026; not in the paper)**
- Frozen ESM2-650M (layer 27), 8-layer decoder (900 d, RMSNorm, QK-norm, gated attention, 198M params), 13,453-term vocabulary (MF 2,501 / BP 9,493 / CC 1,459, GO 2023-01-01), 200 organisms, up to 2,000 residues; trained on 227,965 proteins with "tier-1 ∪ tier-2" labels, 100 epochs on 4x H100, 15% GO-input-token masking, label smoothing 0.03; greedy default — [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2)
- v2 reports mean wF1 0.665 on `proteins/test` (3,726 proteins) and 0.562 on the harder `proteins_extra/test` (6,112 proteins; BP 0.419). Its own limitations list: rare terms never predicted, no conditioning outside 200 organisms, memorises training proteins, frozen to GO 2023-01-01 — [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2)

**BioReason-Pro (the reasoning model)**
- Components: ESM3-1B residue embeddings (layer 38/48, with structure coordinates when available, ~10% of training proteins lack structure); GO graph encoder = 3 GAT layers (8 heads, 512 hidden) over the full GO DAG with Qwen3-4B text-embedding initialisation, compressed to 200 tokens; Qwen3-4B-Thinking backbone with LoRA; text context = organism, InterPro domains with ranges, optional STRING top-10 partners, and greedy GO-GPT predictions — [Paper §4.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [BioReason-Pro RL card (4B)](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/bioreason-pro-rl)
- Data: 133,492 proteins across 3,135 organisms; >130K synthetic reasoning traces generated by GPT-5 from InterPro, UniProt description, organism, localisation, PPI and leaf GO terms; proteins without InterPro domains were dropped from trace training — [Paper §2.1, §4.1.2, B.4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Training: 2-stage SFT on 8x H100 (stage 2: 10 epochs, best at epoch 8); RL = GRPO/GSPO hybrid with Dr.GRPO length regularisation, group size 24, 1,200 steps, ~32 h, ~619M tokens; reward = IA-weighted F1 of propagated GO IDs regex-extracted from the final-answer block; more RL epochs gave no downstream gain — [Paper §4.3, B.4, B.7](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Reported GO numbers: SFT wFmax 0.64 greedy / 0.67 best-of-10; RL 0.66 greedy / 0.67 best-of-10; best unweighted 0.74; evaluated only on no-knowledge proteins because GPT-5 traces leak cross-aspect information for limited-knowledge proteins. Max generation 10,000 tokens, "a few seconds" per protein on vLLM — [Paper §2.4, §4.1.3, §4.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Ablations: removing GO-GPT input weakens annotations; giving the full predicted GO set (with ancestors) beats leaf-only; InterPro in the prompt "consistently improved" performance; GO graph encoder acts as "structured memory" (without it the model forgets/hallucinates GO IDs); Qwen3-8B moderately better than 4B; tree-traversal trace format "performed poorly" — [Paper App. B.4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- Non-GO claims: experts preferred or tied BioReason-Pro vs UniProt in 79% (SFT) / 73% (RL) of 162 proteins; LLM-judge (GPT-5.1) ~8/10 — [Paper §2.5](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)

### Inferences
- GO-GPT's "hierarchy awareness" is only (a) depth-sorted target order and (b) the evaluator's propagation (prop=max) of whatever it emits. Nothing prevents a child without its parent in the raw output; the evaluator repairs it. A model with explicit hierarchy gets no metric credit for consistency on this benchmark.
- BioReason-Pro's GO accuracy is largely inherited from GO-GPT (it consumes GO-GPT's predictions; removing them hurt). Beating GO-GPT on GO metrics is therefore approximately equivalent to beating BioReason-Pro on GO metrics (paper: 0.66 vs 0.65), while BioReason-Pro's distinctive value is the text/explanation channel.
- The public v1 checkpoint (0.687 in our hands) is stronger than BioReason-Pro RL's reported 0.66; that comparison mixes checkpoints and test subsets (BioReason-Pro excludes limited-knowledge proteins), so treat it as indicative only.

### Gaps
- Table S3/S16/S17 contents are images in the HTML; per-aspect values for SFT/RL, exact decoder hyperparameters and FLOP budgets were not extractable. Paper-reported per-aspect GO-GPT values come from our earlier transcription in NOTES.md.
- The `NVIDIA-BioNeMo/BioReason-Pro` GitHub URL in the v2 card returned 404; `bowang-lab/BioReason-Pro` page showed no README content via fetch. Training scripts were not inspected.
- Which checkpoint produced the paper's tables (vs the public one) is not documented.

## Q2. Which other systems are strongest and how do they compare?

### Takeaway
On prospective CAFA-style evaluation the strongest systems are ensembles that fuse homology, PLM embeddings, taxon priors, existing electronic annotations and literature (GOCurator, ProtBoost, InterLabelGO+), all clustered around 0.58-0.60 CAFA-5 wFmax; single-model PLM methods (DeepGO-SE, SPROF-GO, PFresGO, TEMPROT, STAR-GO) report on incompatible benchmarks. No 2024-2026 foundation model (ESM3, ESM C, ProTrek, Evolla) reports CAFA-protocol GO Fmax that would displace these.

### Cited Findings
**CAFA-5 (Kaggle, 2023) and its write-ups**
- Metric: IA-weighted Fmax averaged over MFO/BPO/CCO on no-knowledge + limited-knowledge annotations; 2,760 submissions evaluated; GO 2023-01-01; t0 = 2023-08 — [CAFA5 results preprint (bioRxiv 2026.04.27.716980, preprint)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.27.716980v1.full-text); [CAFA5 evaluation data (Zenodo)](https://zenodo.org/records/20186533)
- 1st: GOCurator (Fudan, Zhu lab), built on NetGO 3.0; components for 3D structure, text descriptions and literature, and GORetrieval/GORetriever, combined by learning-to-rank; reported 5.8% better than 2nd — [GOCurator talk/abstract](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FO7BI2Tud40)
- Adding GORetriever (literature-driven retrieval and re-ranking) to NetGO 3.0 raised CAFA-5 blind-test wFmax from 0.587 to 0.604 — [GORetriever paper](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/5c772562-1734-4dda-a3d2-ddca14e5df68/content)
- 2nd: ProtBoost (team U900), final score 0.58240; 3rd and 4th 0.57276 and 0.56245. Stack: ProtT5 (+ESM2) embeddings, one-hot taxon features, Py-Boost multi-output GBDT, "conditional probability modelling" on the ontology, GCN stacking over the GO graph, electronic GOA annotations as features, parent>=child post-processing — [ProtBoost (arXiv 2412.04529, preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529); [Kaggle write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/cafa-5-protein-function-prediction/writeups/u900-private-2nd-public-5th-solution-py-boost-and-)
- ProtBoost's step-wise ablation: ridge on ProtT5 0.47 → Py-Boost + conditional probabilities 0.51 → taxon features 0.53 → conditional probability models blended 0.55 → electronic GOA annotations 0.57 → NN ensemble 0.59 (GCN stacking: 0.59 → 0.62 internally) — [ProtBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529)
- "About 30% of electronic labels becomes experimental", so IEA labels were used as features rather than as labels — [Kaggle write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/cafa-5-protein-function-prediction/writeups/u900-private-2nd-public-5th-solution-py-boost-and-)
- Other recurring tricks: literature embeddings (Top1, Top4 PROTGOAT), soft-F1 loss (Freddolino lab), restricting to most-annotated proteins and top-frequency targets, merging UniProtKB electronic predictions — [ProtBoost related-work section](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529); [PROTGOAT (bioRxiv, preprint)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.01.587572v1)
- CAFA5 official analysis: non-experimental UniProt annotations were "among the best performing baselines" for Fmax; top-10 methods beat all baselines; the partial-knowledge setting lags; on the subset of annotations unpublished at t0 the top full-evaluation method is usually not the best — [CAFA5 results preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.27.716980v1.full-text)

**InterLabelGO+ (the paper's strongest baseline)**
- ESM2 (last three layers) → MLPs trained with IA-aware weighted-F1 loss + pairwise ranking loss, combined with DIAMOND AlignmentKNN through a dynamic weight driven by top-hit identity/bit-score; CAFA-5 late-submission wFmax 0.5764 (would be 3rd); earlier version 0.5604 — [InterLabelGO+ (Bioinformatics 2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11568131/)
- On the BioReason-Pro temporal test, InterLabelGO+ wFmax 0.63 vs GO-GPT greedy 0.65 — [Paper §2.2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)

**Single-model / academic methods (own benchmarks, not comparable to each other)**
- DeepGO-SE (Nat. Mach. Intell. 2024): ESM2 embeddings projected into several ELEmbedding "world models" of GO axioms, prediction as approximate semantic entailment; Fmax MF 0.554, BP 0.432, CC 0.721 on a Swiss-Prot split where any DIAMOND hit (e<=1e-3) groups proteins; PPI-GAT variant BP 0.435; no homology baselines by design — [DeepGO-SE](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00795-w)
- SPROF-GO (Brief. Bioinform. 2023): ProtT5 + attention pooling + hierarchy-consistent outputs + homology label diffusion; Fmax 0.647/0.335/0.725 (MF/BP/CC) on own split; 0.739/0.453/0.729 on the NetGO 2.0 dataset — [SPROF-GO](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.12.05.519119v1)
- PFresGO (Bioinformatics 2023): attention over GO-term embeddings; Fmax 0.692/0.568/0.674 on its test set — [PFresGO](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article-pdf/doi/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad094/49394696/btad094.pdf)
- TEMPROT (BMC Bioinformatics 2023): ProtBERT-BFD embeddings + homology; CAFA3-derived Fmax BP 0.581, CC 0.692, MF 0.662 — [TEMPROT](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12859-023-05375-0)
- STAR-GO (arXiv 2512.05245, preprint): structure+semantic GO embeddings with zero-shot ability; Fmax BP 0.548, CC 0.659, MF 0.719; DeepGO-SE still best on BP in their table — [STAR-GO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.05245)
- SuperEdgeGO (PLOS Comput. Biol. 2025): edge-supervised structure graphs; Fmax MF 0.801, BP 0.550, CC 0.697 on a human benchmark — [SuperEdgeGO](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1013343)

**Foundation models**
- ProTrek (Nat. Biotechnol. 2025): trimodal sequence/structure/text contrastive model for retrieval, >100x faster than Foldseek/MMseqs2; not reported as a CAFA-protocol GO predictor — [ProTrek](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02836-0)
- GO-GPT authors found ESM3 (no structure) slightly worse and ESM C 600M about equal to ESM2-3B as frozen encoders — [Paper App. B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)

### Inferences
- The CAFA-5 top methods' gains come from information GO-GPT does not use: electronic annotations, literature/text, taxon priors, domain databases and calibrated stacking. GO-GPT authors tried IEA as *labels* (no gain), whereas ProtBoost used IEA as *features* (+0.02); the feature route is untested for GO-GPT.
- The paper's comparison with InterLabelGO+/ProtBoost uses those methods retrained on the same pre-Nov-2022 data; GOCurator (the CAFA-5 winner, literature-heavy) was not compared because no public implementation exists. So "SOTA" in the paper means "best among accessible methods".

### Gaps
- I did not find CAFA-protocol Fmax for ESM3, ESM C, Evolla (bioRxiv 2025 preprint), ProtNLM or other LLM-style annotators; DeepGOPlus numbers were not re-fetched (it is a baseline in DeepGO-SE). ProtBoost's ablation scores are its own leaderboard/CV values, not CAFA5-final.
- CAFA-5 per-team final wFmax per aspect was not extracted from the results preprint (figures).

## Q3. Concrete shortcomings of GO-GPT / BioReason-Pro (with evidence)

### Takeaway
GO-GPT's score is mostly close-homolog recall: it loses to plain DIAMOND + naive and to our HiGO-35M below 50% identity, memorises training proteins, emits uncalibrated binary sets, has a fixed ~10k-term vocabulary, and has clear decoding headroom (best-of-10 oracle +0.05 over greedy). BioReason-Pro adds explanations but at large cost, with hallucinated evidence and organism-label sensitivity, and only ~+0.01 GO wFmax.

### Cited Findings
1. **Dependence on close homologs.** 88% (7,585/8,630) of test proteins have a >=50%-identity DIAMOND hit in the training set. Our per-bin means: >=50% GO-GPT 0.730 vs HiGO+gate 0.599; 30-50% (n=713) 0.440 vs **0.480**; <30% (n=82) 0.395 vs **0.461**; no hit (n=250) 0.374 vs 0.360. DIAMOND + naive also beats GO-GPT below 50% (0.457 vs 0.440; 0.454 vs 0.395) — [REPORT.md §4.1](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/REPORT.md); [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md). The paper itself reports GO-GPT per-protein F1 still correlates with BLAST identity (Spearman ρ=0.46 vs BLAST 0.63) — [Paper §2.4, Table S12](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
2. **Memorisation / leakage.** 96% of CAFA-6 labelled proteins are in GO-GPT's training data with labels; zero-shot on our CAFA-6 split gives 0.897 vs 0.686 on its own temporal holdout — [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md). The v2 card states that training proteins "get memorized rather than predicted annotations" — [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2). Validation is a random 90/10 split of the same pool and validation loss rises after ~20 of 100 epochs — [Paper §4.1, Fig. S9](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full). 760 GO-GPT test proteins appear in training with other aspects (limited-knowledge) — [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md); BioReason-Pro excludes such proteins because cross-aspect leakage inflates scores — [Paper §4.1.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
3. **No calibrated scores; Fmax computed on binary sets.** Greedy assigns 1/0, so "Fmax" is F1 at a single operating point; 10-sample frequencies add +0.02 (0.65→0.67) and the best-of-10 oracle reaches 0.70 — [Paper §2.2, §4.2.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full). Our P/R at Fmax for the public checkpoint: MF P .757 / R .676, BP .631 / .604, CC .684 / .779 — [REPORT.md §8.5](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/REPORT.md)
4. **Autoregressive ordering of an unordered set, exposure bias.** Order is an arbitrary depth sort; training is pure teacher forcing; beam search gave no gain over greedy/sampling; best-of-10 vs greedy gap (0.05) shows probability mass spread over alternative orderings/sets — [Paper §4.2.2-4.2.3, B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full). v2 adds 15% GO-input-token masking, a standard exposure-bias mitigation — [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2). Our greedy vs beam: 0.687 vs 0.686 — [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md)
5. **Vocabulary limits.** Only terms with >=20 training proteins (~10k of ~43k GO terms) can ever be emitted; threshold not ablated; GO fixed at 2023-01-01; v2 card: "rarer terms are never predicted" — [Paper B.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2). Flat tokens share no parameters between a term and its ancestors (unlike HiGO's ancestor-mean queries) — [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md)
6. **Organism handling.** Only 200 organisms have embeddings; others are "unknown" — [GO-GPT v2 card](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/gogpt-v2). For BioReason-Pro, the same EvoAcr1 sequence was predicted as ribosomal protein, transcriptional repressor, membrane effector or translation initiation factor depending on the organism label — [Paper §2.7](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
7. **Hallucinated evidence (BioReason-Pro).** For short peptides lacking domains the SFT model fabricated InterPro entries and built mechanistic narratives on them; GPT-5 trace generation failed without domains, so domain-less proteins were removed from trace training — [Paper §2.7, B.4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
8. **Reward/metric coupling.** RL reward is IA-weighted F1 on regex-extracted IDs; extending RL gave no downstream gain although on-policy reward rose; authors note the reward does not measure explanatory quality — [Paper §2.4, §3, B.4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
9. **Cost.** GO-GPT v1: ESM2-3B forward + autoregressive decode; our full 8,630-protein run took ~1.9 h on one H100 with 3 processes — [NOTES.md](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/NOTES.md). BioReason-Pro: ESM3-1B + Qwen3-4B, up to 10,000 generated tokens, "a few seconds" per protein, RL ~32 h on 8 H100 — [Paper §4.3, B.7](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
10. **Ignored evidence channels.** GO-GPT uses no InterPro, homology retrieval, IEA or text even though InterPro helped BioReason-Pro and IEA-as-features helped ProtBoost (+0.02) — [Paper B.4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full); [ProtBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529)
11. **Our own counter-evidence (limits of the exploit).** Our grounded calibrator (InterPro/orthologs/ligands) is 0.541 on CAFA-6 clustered test but drops to 0.550 vs 0.576 for the plain gate on the GO-GPT temporal test, because it was fit on a random split and overweights ortholog GOA (0.7-0.8) — [REPORT.md §8.4](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/REPORT.md)

### Inferences
- **IA-weighting misalignment (inference).** GO-GPT is trained with unweighted token CE, so it is optimised for frequent shallow terms; its unweighted-vs-weighted gap (0.76 vs 0.65 in the paper) is larger than InterLabelGO+'s (0.70 vs 0.63), consistent with over-producing low-IA terms. An IA-weighted objective or IA-aware thresholding is a direct lever.
- **Calibration as an exploitable gap (inference).** Because GO-GPT's public output is a set, any stack that turns it into scores (sampling frequency, beam/position features, agreement with kNN) and then picks a per-aspect threshold should gain ~0.02 for free, as the paper's own sampling result shows.
- **Hierarchy inconsistency is not penalised on this benchmark (inference).** The evaluator propagates predictions, so hierarchy-consistent output helps only via better ranking, not via the metric. Our data agree: the hierarchy constraint keeps only +0.004 after the gate (REPORT §8.7).
- **Temporal mismatch (inference).** Temporal test labels are "next annotations", often specific BP terms; GO-GPT's BP jump from 0.539 (paper) to 0.617 (public checkpoint) suggests BP is the most checkpoint-/data-sensitive aspect.

### Gaps
- No measurement yet of how much test IA mass lies outside GO-GPT's ~10k-term vocabulary, or how often GO-GPT's raw outputs violate the hierarchy. Both are cheap to compute with `gogpt_eval.py` outputs and would quantify shortcomings 4-5.
- No per-organism or per-IA-bin breakdown of GO-GPT on its test set yet.

## Q4. Where is the headroom: which strata must a new model win to beat 0.687 overall?

### Takeaway
The >=50%-identity bin (88% of proteins) decides the benchmark. Even our full remote-homology advantage moves the overall score by only about +0.004 (inference), so a new model must essentially match GO-GPT on close homologs (within ~0.005-0.01 wFmax) and win BP, where our gap is largest (0.434 vs 0.617).

### Cited Findings
- Stratum sizes and means: >=50% n=7,585 (GO-GPT 0.730), 30-50% n=713 (0.440), <30% n=82 (0.395), no hit n=250 (0.374) — [REPORT.md §4.1](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/REPORT.md)
- Aspect gaps (GO-GPT vs HiGO+gate): MF 0.714 vs 0.647, BP 0.617 vs 0.434, CC 0.728 vs 0.647; DIAMOND + naive 0.650/0.416/0.638 — [REPORT.md §4.1](file:///home/aj0486@students.ad.unt.edu/PFA/cafa6/REPORT.md)
- Test composition: BP 5,819 proteins / 73.4% of annotations, MF 2,080, CC 3,440; the reported mean weights aspects equally — [Paper §4.1.3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- GO-GPT's own decoding headroom: greedy 0.65 → sampled frequencies 0.67 → oracle 0.70 — [Paper §2.2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.19.712954v1.full)
- CAFA-5 style gains from extra information: IEA-as-feature +0.02, taxon +0.02, literature retrieval +0.017 — [ProtBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529); [GORetriever](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/5c772562-1734-4dda-a3d2-ddca14e5df68/content)

### Inferences
- **Back-of-envelope decomposition (inference; Fmax is not exactly decomposable).** A protein-count-weighted average of the bin means gives (7,585×0.730 + 713×0.440 + 82×0.395 + 250×0.374)/8,630 ≈ 0.693, close to the measured 0.687, so the approximation is usable. Replacing GO-GPT by HiGO+gate on the three non-close bins changes the total by (713×0.040 + 82×0.066 − 250×0.014)/8,630 ≈ +0.0035. Each +0.01 in the >=50% bin is worth ≈ +0.0088 overall.
- **Implication (inference).** Winning remote homologs is necessary for a scientific claim but nearly irrelevant to the headline number. To pass 0.687 a model needs roughly ≥0.725 on the >=50% bin while keeping its remote-homology lead. Our current >=50% score (0.599) is 0.13 short; most of that is BP.
- **Most promising levers, ranked (inference):**
  1. *Close-homolog BP/CC recall*: a stronger kNN/label-transfer channel (multiple hits, identity- and coverage-weighted, taxon-aware) plus a larger backbone (ESM2-650M is reported to be close to 3B for GO-GPT). This is where GO-GPT earns its lead.
  2. *Stacking on GO-GPT itself*: use GO-GPT sampled frequencies as a feature and calibrate thresholds per aspect (+0.02 shown by the paper alone). Legitimate on its benchmark if GO-GPT is treated as a frozen feature; it will not transfer to CAFA-6 because of the 96% leak.
  3. *Time-aware evidence*: IEA/InterPro2GO/ortholog-GOA features fit on a temporal validation split (our calibrator failed exactly because its validation split was random).
  4. *IA-weighted objective and rare/out-of-vocabulary terms*: gains concentrate in high-IA BP terms that a ≥20-protein flat vocabulary cannot emit.
  5. *Organisms outside the 200*: a stratum where GO-GPT has no conditioning; size on the test set still to be measured.
- **Strata to report** for any new model: identity bins (as above), aspect, IA tercile of the protein's labels, in-vocabulary vs out-of-vocabulary labels, organism in/out of GO-GPT's 200, and no-knowledge vs limited-knowledge proteins.

### Gaps
- Exact stratum-level Fmax per aspect for GO-GPT (not just the mean) is not in the notes; recomputing per aspect per bin would sharpen the BP vs MF/CC targeting.
- Organism and vocabulary strata are unmeasured.
